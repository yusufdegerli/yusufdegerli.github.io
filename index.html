fig.02 — raycaster.js
click, then W A S D · drag to look

Raycasting engine

One ray per screen column, stepped through the grid with DDA. The perpendicular hit distance removes the fisheye effect and sets wall height; the minimap draws the actual rays being cast. My original engine, cub3D, is written in C with hand-managed memory. This is the same algorithm, re-written for the browser.

fig.03 — verlet.js
drag to throw · click to add

2D physics

Verlet integration on a fixed 120 Hz step, decoupled from the display. Collisions and the chain’s distance constraints are relaxed iteratively, weighted by mass. Velocity is implicit (position minus previous position), so drag-and-throw needs no extra code. The tick on each body is that velocity.

fig.01 ↑

Cellular-automata liquid

The header runs 19,200 cells updated bottom-up every tick. The scan direction alternates to avoid drift, and each cell keeps a flow direction so water travels in sheets instead of jittering. Water falls, slides diagonally, then disperses sideways. Overflow drains off the right edge.

for each water cell, bottom-up:
  if   below empty    → fall
  elif diagonal empty → slide
  else                → spread ≤ 4
  if   off-grid       → drain

Document-inspection sim · DOM + CSS, no canvas

Galactic Customs Inspector

Six shifts at an alien border post. Every traveller brings a generated passport, a spoken reason for visiting and a four-character alias. Approve, deny or quarantine them against a rulebook that grows each day, before a 25-second timer runs out.

  • Rules are data. Each day’s rulebook is a list of objects with a violates(traveler) predicate, so judging, generation and the rulebook UI all read one source of truth.
  • Decidable by construction. Travellers are generated valid, then 40% get exactly one injected violation, so every case has a single correct stamp.
  • No images, no canvas. Nine alien species are drawn in pure CSS, the desk uses pointer-captured drag and drop, and every sound is synthesised with Web Audio.
  • Vanilla JS
  • CSS art
  • Web Audio
  • Pointer events

Top-down heist action · Canvas 2D

Cold Column: The Istanbul Job

A one-hit-kill heist across two floors of the Istanbul Mint. Seven weapons you can also throw, doors you can slam, glass that shatters, a combo meter, and dialogue scenes between floors.

  • Guards perceive the world. An 83° view cone with a 420 px range, ray-tested for line of sight against every wall, plus hearing radii per noise type, from gunshots (560 px) to footsteps (108 px).
  • Patrol → search → combat. Search routes come from A* over a 40 px navigation grid stored in typed arrays, re-planned roughly every 0.4 s.
  • Physical props. Doors are hinged with angular velocity and damping, so slamming one stuns a guard. Bullets test glass along their path. Music streams Creative Commons tracks, with a Web Audio synth score as fallback.

Local 2-player car soccer · Planck.js (Box2D)

RIMWRECK

Two players on one keyboard, five vehicles, one ball, first to five goals. Cars shed bumpers, hoods and roofs as they take hits, and pickups shrink, grow, reverse or arm them, or resize a goal.

  • Real suspension. Each vehicle is a multi-fixture rigid body on spring WheelJoints tuned per vehicle (2.7–7.4 Hz), driven by wheel motors, with torque control in mid-air.
  • Damage from impulses. A post-solve contact listener measures relative normal velocity at each contact. Hard enough impacts detach body parts as separate physics debris.
  • Instant replay. A 3.6-second ring buffer of frame snapshots replays every goal at 0.3× speed. The physics step is fixed at 60 Hz, separate from rendering.
  • Planck.js
  • Rigid-body physics

About

I learned to program at 42 Istanbul: no lectures and no frameworks, just C, C++, a Unix shell and relentless peer review.

Writing shells, IRC servers and concurrent simulations taught me to care about memory, latency and failure modes. I bring that to the browser: interfaces that stay responsive under real data, Canvas graphics that hold their frame rate, and real-time features that degrade gracefully.

Today I split my time between evaluating frontier models on hard software-engineering tasks at xAI, designing the prompts and benchmarks that expose their edge-case failures, and building production software, from hotel booking engines to restaurant POS systems.
